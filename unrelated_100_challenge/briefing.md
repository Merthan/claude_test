# "100 to 100" (Science & Startups Berlin): what they are looking for, and how to write the 100 words

*Written 31 October 2026. The challenge page itself and FU Berlin's announcements could not be opened from this session; the facts below come from the page text you pasted, from search summaries of the FU announcements, and from how comparable university idea competitions judge. Items marked "verify" are not confirmed.*

## The facts

- **What it is.** A monthly idea competition run by Science & Startups, the joint startup service of FU, HU, TU and Charité. You describe a startup idea in 100 words. Each month the **startup consultants at Science & Startups** pick the five best and pay €100 each. Monthly winners qualify for the yearly "Brilliant 1000", where the five best ideas get another €1,000 each (finalists expand the idea into a 1,000-word one-pager plus a short video pitch; the 2025 finale was 13 November 2025 at the Max-Liebermann-Haus).
- **Fields.** "All fields: new molecules, climate action, health, social innovation, the environment, education or culture." Education is on the list by name.
- **The one condition.** "What matters is that the idea was developed within Berlin's research community." As a TU Berlin student and graduate you qualify; say so where the form asks for affiliation, and if there is no such field, spend four words on it in the text.
- **Form.** "Describe your startup idea in 100 words." The FU announcement says "exactly 100 words" (verify on the form). Submit through the Match'em page or by email to 100to100@science-startups.berlin. Language: the page and the FU announcement are in English; English is safe, German is almost certainly accepted too (verify on the form). Required fields beyond the text: not stated; expect name, affiliation, contact, maybe a title.
- **Confidentiality.** Submissions are treated confidentially, nothing is published without consent, data is not passed on. There is no reason to hold back the product.
- **The hidden prize.** "Every participant can also book a free consultation with our startup experts." That consultation is worth more than the €100: it is the direct route to ask Science & Startups about the Berliner Startup Stipendium Batch V (January to February 2027 application, April start), the team-size rule, and EXIST. Book it the day you submit, win or not.

## Who reads it, and what that means

The readers are startup consultants, not scientists and not a jury of professors. They read hundreds of these and they run the incubation programmes. They are, consciously or not, screening for people they want in those programmes. So the 100 words are judged the way a coach judges a first conversation:

1. **Is there a real problem, stated so I feel it?** One concrete sentence, ideally with a "why now".
2. **Is the solution clear in one sentence?** No feature list, no technology names.
3. **Is there something hard or new in it?** Something a weekend app cannot do. Your on-device speech coach is that thing; the clicker is not.
4. **Who is it for and is it big?** Students, lecturers, anyone who presents; universities as the channel.
5. **Does it make the world better?** Their own phrase. For you: rehearsal feedback for everyone, not only people who can pay a coach; privacy by design; speaking skills in the AI era; fewer throwaway devices.
6. **Is this person already doing it?** One clause of traction beats three adjectives: "a predecessor reached 46,000 installs; prototypes run on iOS, Android, Windows and macOS."

Comparable university competitions (Science4Life, the Graz Science Park competition) publish criteria along the same lines: degree of innovation, customer benefit and market, feasibility, impact or sustainability, team. The page lists none; assume these.

## Which of your three angles to lead with

| Angle | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Environmental ("25 million clickers, 4,000 tonnes of waste") | **Closing clause, not the lead** | The numbers are not sourced and a coach who asks "where is that from?" gets no answer. Clickers are also not a problem anyone feels. "Millions of single-purpose clickers are sold every year and soon thrown away" is defensible and still makes the point. Leading with it reads as green dressing on a software idea; closing with it reads as a bonus |
| "So much more is possible while presenting" | **Good hook, weak on its own** | "The best device is already in your pocket" is a line readers remember, but it has to be followed by what the phone does that a clicker cannot, and quickly by the coach |
| Classrooms and universities shift to in-person presentations because of AI, yet the tools are bad and nobody gets feedback | **The lead** | It is a "why now" that every university consultant has heard in their own meetings this year; it is in their "education" field; it makes the coach the point rather than a feature; and it frames the impact (speaking skills, equal access to practice, privacy) in their language |
| The coach as science (compressed model, eight languages, on device, nothing uploaded) | **The proof paragraph** | For a science-and-startups audience, one concrete technical claim is worth more than ten adjectives. "A speech model we halved in size at identical accuracy transcribes eight languages on the device" is checkable and unusual |

Recommended shape: lead with the shift, hook with the pocket device, prove with the coach, close with traction and impact. Draft 1 in `drafts.md` does this; draft 2 leads with the pocket device; draft 3 is your environmental lead rewritten to be defensible; draft 4 is the science-forward version; draft 5 is draft 1 in German.

## A word budget for 100 words

| Beat | Words | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Why now and the problem | 25 to 30 | AI pushes assessment back into the room; presenters use a €10 clicker and get no feedback |
| The product in one sentence | 20 to 25 | the phone becomes remote, laser, spotlight, audience polls, and a private speaking coach |
| The hard part and the privacy | 20 to 25 | on-device speech analysis, eight languages, nothing uploaded, shows where pace, pauses and fillers lose the room |
| Traction | 12 to 15 | predecessor 46,000 installs; prototypes on all four platforms |
| Impact, as the last line | 6 to 10 | rehearsal feedback for everyone; no hardware; nothing leaves the phone |

## Style rules that matter here

- Present tense, concrete nouns, one idea per sentence. No "revolutionary", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "AI-powered".
- No technology names a consultant will not know: no HID, GATT, relay, Bluetooth profiles, Cloudflare, quantization. "A speech model we halved in size" is the limit.
- Numbers only where you can defend them: 46,000 installs (true), eight languages (true), half the size at identical accuracy (true per your measurements), €10 clicker (true). Not the waste tonnage.
- Fillers are English-only in the product; "pace, pauses and fillers" is still honest as a description of what the coach shows. Do not claim fillers in eight languages.
- Do not mention stipends, funding, or that you are applying elsewhere.
- "Nothing uploaded" is the one promise they will remember; keep it in every draft.
- If the form has a title field: "AnyPresent: your phone as remote, audience and speaking coach".
- Count the words in the form's own counter; hyphenated words and numbers count differently across tools. All drafts were counted at exactly 100 with a standard counter.

## After you submit

- Book the free consultation immediately and bring the questions from `response3/04-stipends-and-money-reassessed.md`: Batch V dates, whether a solo founder is accepted, whether a free public release before the start matters, and EXIST's view of the speech pipeline.
- Match'em is also their co-founder matching platform. If the team-size rule turns out to be real, a profile there is where a business or education co-founder is most likely to find you.
- If you win a month, the Brilliant 1000 needs a 1,000-word one-pager and a short video; both come straight out of the stipend application material. Mention the win, or even the submission, in the Batch V application.
