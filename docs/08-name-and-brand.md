# AnyPresent briefing 08: Name and brand

## Short answer

Keep AnyPresent. It is a solid 7 out of 10: available, pronounceable in English and German, short enough for a watermark, and it carries the right promise ("present from anything, to anything, always"). Its weaknesses (the gift reading of "present", a slightly generic "Any-" prefix) are real but minor, and the store subtitle, not the name, does the discovery work. Changing names now would cost more in time and domain and trademark checks than it could possibly return. Confidence: high.

## The name, assessed

For it:
- **Available.** You own anypresent.com, and TMview and the DPMA register show nothing for it. That alone eliminates most alternatives; "Slide" names are taken, as you found.
- **Says the promise.** "Any" is the always-connects story: any slide software, any computer, any phone, any connection. That is the product's actual differentiator, and the name repeats it every time someone says it.
- **Reads as software.** AnyDesk, AnyList, AnyTrans: the prefix signals a utility. AnyDesk in particular is a remote-control tool, which is the right neighbourhood.
- **Watermark-friendly.** Ten characters, fourteen as a URL. Legible in a corner of a projected slide from the back of a room, which is what the strategy needs.
- **Works in German.** No awkward sound or meaning; "Ennipräsent" is fine for teachers.

Against it:
- **"Present" is also a gift.** "AnyPresent" can read as a gifting app in a search-results list, and some people will say it with the noun's stress. The icon and subtitle fix this at a glance. In store search it does not matter, because nobody searches for "anypresent" except people who already know it.
- **Not descriptive.** The name does not say "remote", "clicker" or "slides". The App Store title and subtitle must carry those words (doc 03).
- **Generic prefix.** "Any-" is common and unprotectable; your brand is the full word, not the prefix.
- **A small risk of confusion** with unrelated "AnyPresent" uses in other categories over time. Acceptable.

Verdict: keep it. Spend no further hours on naming.

## Store naming

- **Apple**: title "AnyPresent: Slide Remote" (fits 30 characters), subtitle "Clicker, laser & spotlight". Apple's rule 2.3.7 forbids other apps' names and trademarks in the title, subtitle and screenshots, so "PowerPoint" and "Keynote" cannot appear there; they can appear in the description and the keyword field, and compatibility can be shown with neutral slides in screenshots.
- **Google Play**: title within 30 characters; the old listing's "Bluetooth Presenter" phrase has search history, so "AnyPresent: Bluetooth Presenter" if it fits the limit, otherwise "AnyPresent Bluetooth Presenter" or keep "Presenter" in the short description.
- **Companion**: "AnyPresent Companion" in the tray, "AnyPresent for Mac" and "AnyPresent for Windows" on the download page. Microsoft Store listing under the same name.
- **Website surfaces**: anypresent.com/present (viewer), anypresent.com/remote (web remote), anypresent.com/get (companion), anypresent.com/business.
- **Publisher**: "Appless" is a nice nod to "nothing to install" and is fine as a trade name. Legal pages must show your real name while you are a sole proprietor (doc 09).

## The watermark, as a design brief

- Text: "anypresent.com". The URL, not the name, because viewers can act on it and it reads as a credit rather than a nag.
- Placement: bottom corner opposite the spotlight's usual position, small (about the height of a slide's footer text), slightly transparent, with a tiny mark (the app icon's shape) in front.
- Behaviour: fades in with the effect, fades out with it, never pulses, never moves, never overlays slide text if it can avoid it, never appears on the phone or in keyboard mode.
- Tone: a feature credit, like a camera's logo in a photo corner. If it looks like a trial-software nag, students stop using effects and you lose both the spread and the reason to pay.
- Business: the same spot becomes "Presented with [their logo]" in a future business feature, which turns the watermark into something people pay to keep rather than to remove.

## Product family naming

If the second product (keyboard and mouse) happens, keep the family: "AnyPresent" for presentations and a sibling built on the same "Any-" pattern for the keyboard app. Do not use "AnyPresent Keyboard"; Apple's 4.3 rule on near-duplicates is easier to satisfy when the names and the icons say "different product".

## Trademark and domains

- A German word mark at the DPMA costs €290 for up to three classes when filed electronically; class 9 (software) and class 42 (software services) are the ones that matter. An EU mark at the EUIPO costs €850 for one class. Not needed before launch; file the DPMA mark when revenue arrives or when the stipend's material budget can pay for it. The point is defensive: it stops a later entrant from taking the name on the stores.
- Domains: anypresent.com is enough. Register anypresent.de only if it is cheap and free, mainly to catch typed German traffic; do not chase .app or .io.
- Social handles: take "anypresent" on the platforms you will post video to, before launch, even if you post nothing yet.

## Visual identity, the minimum

One icon that reads at 60 pixels: a phone or a pointer dot with a soft spotlight circle. One accent colour used for the laser and the spotlight edge in every screenshot and clip, so the effect itself becomes recognisable. A light and a dark store screenshot set. No mascot, no gradient logo, no further time.

## What I am not sure about

- **Whether the gift reading of "present" hurts in the US** more than I estimate. If early US reviews or support emails show confusion, the fix is the subtitle, not the name.
- **The exact title strings.** Store character limits and Apple's keyword de-duplication rules change; test the strings in App Store Connect and the Play Console before committing.
- **Trademark classes.** Confirm class 9 and 42 with the DPMA's free guidance before filing; I am not a trademark adviser.
