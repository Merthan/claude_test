# AnyPresent briefing 07: Startup or tool, and the stipend question

## Short answer

AnyPresent is a tool business. It is not venture-fundable and you should not try to make it look like one. It is, however, a perfectly normal candidate for EXIST or the Berliner Startup Stipendium, which fund hundreds of small software ventures every year. For your stated goal (mostly income, possibly a company), a stipend is the single biggest lever in year one: €12,000 to €45,000 of guaranteed money against an expected €5,000 of app revenue. So: pursue a stipend now, in a way that does not stop you building, and let the rules (which you must confirm this month) decide whether paid features wait. Confidence: high on the recommendation, medium on the rule details.

## Why the stipend question dominates year one

Doc 01's Base case earns about €5,000 in the first year and €18,000 in the second. EXIST at the graduate rate pays €30,000 over twelve months plus up to €10,000 for costs and €5,000 for coaching. Berlin pays up to €30,000. Even the student rate (€12,000 plus extras) exceeds the Base case. A 55% chance at €30,000 to €45,000 is worth €16,000 to €25,000 in expectation, which is more than the entire first-year revenue of the Base case several times over. No pricing decision, feature or marketing channel moves the needle like that. The cost is a few weeks of application work and possibly a delay in switching on paid features.

## The facts, as verified on 7 October 2026

### EXIST Gründungsstipendium (federal, via your university)

- Solo founders and teams of up to three. At least one founder needs a degree. Teams made mostly of students are funded only in exceptional cases.
- Monthly: €1,000 for students who have completed at least half their coursework, €2,500 for graduates (degree within the last five years), €3,000 for PhDs. Twelve months. Plus up to €10,000 for material costs for a solo founder and €5,000 for coaching.
- The company must not have been founded, and business activity must not have started, at project start. The company is expected to be founded during or after the funding period.
- "Modifications of existing products without a significant unique selling point" are excluded.
- Applications go through a university or research institution with a founder network; you cannot apply alone. A mentor professor is part of the application.
- No other living-cost stipend, no employment above five hours a week, no ventures that are freelance work in substance.
- The programme runs to the end of 2029 and accepts applications continuously. The current guideline text is the April 2023 version. Decision times at Projektträger Jülich are typically two to four months after the university submits; that figure is from experience, not from the sources.
- Your figure of roughly 55% approval of submitted applications is plausible; universities also filter before submitting.

### Berliner Startup Stipendium (state, via incubators)

- Up to €2,500 a month per person, typically twelve months (some sources describe longer ranges), paid through incubators that run their own selection and programme.
- The second funding call started 1 March 2026 with about €31 million for about 680 participants. Batch IV applications run October to November 2026 for a 1 January 2027 start. Batch V runs January to February 2027 for a 1 April 2027 start. These dates come from the Senate's April 2026 overview; confirm them with the incubator.
- A degree is the usual condition (your Bachelor's counts). Some incubators prefer or require teams of two or three; some accept solo founders. Not founded yet, Berlin seat, not after EXIST. Some incubators require a consultation before applying.
- Incubators include Science & Startups (FU, HU, TU, Charité) and HWR's Startup Incubator Berlin; your own university's incubator is the first place to ask.

### What both share

- No founded company, no started business activity, before the start date.
- One or the other, not both, for the same venture.
- Full-time commitment expected.

## The three unknowns that decide your sequencing (ask this month)

1. **Does a free public launch count as "business activity"?** My reading: a free app with no revenue and no company is not "Geschäftstätigkeit" in the sense of the rules, and traction from it strengthens an application. But reviewers differ, and only your Gründungsservice (or an email to PtJ) can tell you. If the answer is "a free launch is fine, revenue is not", hold paid features until the start date. If the answer is "no public launch at all", see the decision tree below.
2. **Which EXIST rate applies to you?** You hold a Bachelor's and are enrolled in a Master's. Many universities treat a completed degree as qualifying for the graduate rate (€2,500) even while enrolled further; some classify by current enrolment (€1,000). The difference is €18,000. Ask, and ask whether de-registering from the Master's would change it, and what that does to your health insurance and student status.
3. **How does your old app's revenue history read?** You have sold a product since 2021 without a registered business (doc 09). Present it as prior market validation of a different, discontinued product, not as the venture's business activity, and be transparent. Ask whether it is a problem; I expect "no, but register properly now".

## Decision tree with hard dates

1. **This week**: email or call the founder service of the university where you are enrolled. Ask the questions at the end of this document. Ask which Berlin incubator you belong to and whether Batch IV is still open for you.
2. **If solo founders are accepted and Batch IV is open**: apply to the Berliner Startup Stipendium now (deadline likely in November; it needs a short business plan, a prototype you already have, and a consultation). Decision before Christmas, start 1 January 2027. This is the fastest money.
3. **In parallel or if Berlin says no**: prepare the EXIST Ideenpapier through the university with a mentor professor in November and December, including the free launch's first numbers. Decision around March to April 2027, start April to July.
4. **Launch the free version in December or January regardless**, unless the Gründungsservice says a public launch disqualifies you. Closed beta on TestFlight and Play closed testing is not a public launch and is safe in any reading.
5. **Paid features**: switch on at stipend start. If Berlin rejects and EXIST is pending, keep holding only if the service confirmed revenue would hurt. Hard ceiling: switch on paid features by 1 June 2027 no matter what, so the stipend process cannot delay revenue indefinitely.
6. **If the answer to unknown 1 is "no public launch at all"**: wait if the graduate rate applies (the expected value of the stipend exceeds the cost of a four-to-six-month delay in this niche, where nothing moves fast); at the student rate it is a coin flip and I would lean toward launching and forgoing the stipend. In the waiting case, run the biggest closed beta you can, and finish the companion, the web viewer and the second product's groundwork meanwhile.

## What would make it more fundable

In rough order of effect on a jury:

1. **A second person.** EXIST funds solo founders but prefers teams, and a solo technical founder with no marketing partner is the most common weakness in software applications. Your university's founder service usually runs co-founder matching; a part-time business or education co-founder, even at 20%, changes the application more than any feature. You said nobody is available; still ask the service.
2. **Evidence.** The old app's 46,000 installs and 2.6% conversion are real market validation. The free launch's first weeks add current numbers. Two or three letters of intent from a university teaching centre, a teacher, or a trainer cost an email each and count a lot.
3. **A unique selling point stated as architecture, not features.** "Always connects: five paths, automatic fallback, nothing to install" and "the €130 Logitech Spotlight experience from the phone you already own". That is clearly not a modification of the old app; say so plainly and describe the old app as the predecessor that proved demand.
4. **A business model with more than €9.99 one-time purchases.** Business and classroom licenses on the desktop side (doc 04), the web viewer for institutions, the second product sharing the core. Juries want to see a path to a company, not a lifestyle app, and you can describe that path honestly without promising venture scale.
5. **A plausible larger frame.** "The delivery layer for presentations: remote, effects, notes, transcripts, team licensing, across every slide tool" is true to the roadmap and is enough for a stipend jury. It is not enough for an investor and you should not try to make it so.

## How to frame the old app

Not "I am relaunching my 2021 app". Instead: "In 2021 I validated demand for phone-as-presenter with a single-platform prototype that reached 46,000 installs and 2.6% paid conversion with no marketing, and learned that connection reliability and the missing iPhone version were the limits. AnyPresent is a new product built on that evidence: cross-platform, a new connection architecture, on-screen effects, a web fallback, and business licensing." Every sentence is true, and it turns the exclusion risk into your strongest slide.

## Startup or tool, as a working stance

Call it a tool business that uses startup instruments to fund its first year. Concretely:
- Register a sole proprietorship now (doc 09); found a UG or GmbH only if a stipend requires it or revenue justifies it, at the end of the funding period.
- No pitch deck for investors, no fundraising. If someone offers to buy the business in two years, that is the exit.
- If the stipend comes with incubator obligations (workshops, reporting), accept them as the price of a paid year and do not let them eat more than a day a week.

## Questions for the Gründungsservice (copy and paste)

1. I hold a Bachelor's in Wirtschaftsinformatik and am enrolled in a Master's without attending courses. For EXIST, am I a student (€1,000) or a graduate (€2,500)? Would de-registering change that, and what else would it change?
2. Since 2021 I have sold a small Android app (about €50 to €100 a month, no registered business). Does that count as business activity already started? How should I present it?
3. If I release the new product for free (no revenue, no company) before the funding starts, does that affect eligibility? What if I enable paid features before the start?
4. Is a solo founder realistic here? Do you offer co-founder matching?
5. Which professor could act as mentor for a software product in this area?
6. What is the current timeline from Ideenpapier to decision to start?
7. For the Berliner Startup Stipendium: which incubator fits me, is Batch IV still open, do they accept solo founders, is a consultation required, and can I apply to both programmes in parallel and choose one?
8. Would EXIST consider a cross-platform rebuild with a new architecture a "modification of an existing product"? How do you advise framing it?
9. What counts as material costs (devices, developer accounts, code-signing certificates, a watch)?
10. Health insurance and student status implications of each path.

## What I am not sure about

- **Whether a free public launch counts as business activity.** This is the question that decides your sequencing, and only the programme can answer it.
- **Your EXIST rate.** I lean toward "graduate" because you hold a degree, with low confidence.
- **Whether applying to Berlin and EXIST in parallel is acceptable.** I believe you may apply to both and accept one, but confirm.
- **Berlin incubators' stance on solo founders.** It varies by incubator and batch.
- **Decision times.** Two to four months is typical; it can be longer.
- **Whether the old app's unregistered revenue creates a problem** beyond "register now". I expect not, but I am not a tax or grant adviser.
