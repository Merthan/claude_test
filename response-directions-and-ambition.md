# Directions, ambition, and the funded-year plan

## Why aim higher, and the one rule

Juries fund credible ambition, and the bigger directions are also where the money ceiling moves from "€2,000 a month" to "a company". The rule: keep two numbers for everything, the aim you write in the application and the expectation you plan your life around. Both are below. The aims are not decoration: the two biggest new directions, room mode and event mode, are also the two I most want you to build for spread.

## The directions that matter, each in a paragraph or two

### Group mode

Students present in groups of three to five and pass the clicker around. "Everyone in the group gets the clicker on their phone" is an exact fit: several controllers in one session, an optional "whose turn" lock, a guest path through the web remote for the member without the app. Every group member installs or joins, which is the most direct spread mechanism available to you, and it is visible in the room. Cost: a session model with several controllers, which the protocol should have anyway. Free forever. Phase 1.

### Room mode for institutions

The lecture-hall PC is the room students fear and the one where nothing can be installed. Flip it: the university's IT installs the companion once on its lecture PCs in a managed mode (no tray UI, auto-start, a room name, a pairing code shown on demand or printed on a sticker at the lectern, MSI and PKG packages, a privacy sheet). Every lecturer and student then connects by code from any phone. What the institution gets: an end to lost, stolen and dead clickers, which is a real nuisance for AV support; effects on every screen; a privacy-clean tool with no accounts. What you get: your credit on every lecture screen in the building, and a site-license product. The free institutional tier shows the toast; the paid tier (€1,000 to €5,000 a year per campus is a guess) removes it and adds an admin page. Berlin alone has TU, HU, FU, HTW, HWR, BHT and Charité within tram distance for pilots, and a stipend year is exactly when you can afford ten pilot conversations. Sales cycles are slow, six to eighteen months, so start conversations in month one and ship managed mode in Phase 3. Two letters of intent from teaching or IT units are also the strongest thing you can put in an application.

### Transcription, recap, and live captions

On-device speech recognition on the companion (macOS and Windows both have system engines, and whisper-class models run locally) aligned to slide changes gives you four things from one pipeline: a transcript per slide, an exportable recap ("what I said on each slide"), rehearsal analytics, and live captions drawn as an overlay for any slide tool. PowerPoint and Google Slides have built-in captions; Keynote and PDF viewers do not; a companion-level overlay covers everything, and accessibility obligations at universities are tightening. It is privacy-clean because nothing leaves the machine. This is the Pro+ subscription feature and the pitch line that makes a jury see a product rather than a clicker. Risk: recognition quality with a distant microphone; the presenter's own laptop mic is close enough for the presenter's own voice, which is all you need. Phase 2.

### Event mode

Academic conferences and meetups have a stage laptop, a session chair waving a paper sign at speakers, and speakers who walk up with a USB stick. Event mode: the organizer installs the companion on the stage laptop, each speaker connects by code, the chair's phone or a web page shows a timer and sends "five minutes left" to the speaker's phone or watch as a vibration. Organizers pay per event (€20 to €50, a guess) or get it free in year one; speakers get the app for free, and speakers are exactly the people who present often. Phase 3, and the first pilot can be a single conference at your own university.

### Audience slide-following and questions

Attendees scan a QR on the screen, see the current slide on their phone (from the viewer's PDF or the companion's capture), submit questions that appear on the presenter's phone, and react. This is the Mentimeter and Slido space, crowded and valuable at tens of millions of euros a year; your angle is "add it to any deck without editing the deck". The growth loop is every attendee seeing the brand, and some of them present next week. The cost is high (real-time fan-out, moderation, abuse), so it is the last Phase 3 item and may slip to year two. For the application it is the natural "where this goes" slide. Do not build polls; slide-following plus questions is enough to be useful and different.

### Video-call positioning

If the spotlight and magnifier overlay is captured by screen sharing (test it: "share screen" in Zoom and Teams captures overlays, "share window" may not), then every remote presentation is a use case: point at your shared slides with your phone, stand up, walk, never alt-tab. There are far more presentations in calls than in rooms, and the credit appears inside the shared screen. Cost: a test and a page. Phase 1 marketing.

### Rehearsal analytics

Time per slide, total time, pace in words per minute, long pauses, filler-word counts, "you spent 40% of the time on slide 3". Analytics, not judgment, so it is reliable and defensible, and it is the student's private tool, which fits the no-accounts posture. Students rehearse at home with the phone alone; timing needs no companion and pace needs only the phone's microphone. PowerPoint's Presenter Coach serves PowerPoint users only. Phase 2, cheap once speech recognition exists.

### Keyboard and mouse sibling

A separate product that shares the core, with roughly 25 times the market and a weak iOS incumbent. In the application, mention it as a product-line extension of the same company ("nothing-to-install input from the phone you own"), not as a second project; juries dislike split focus. Decide the start date at day 90 as before, or earlier if the core is clean.

### Fun pointers and cosmetics

See the monetization file. Cheap, social-video-friendly, and the only thing on this list that could make a meme.

## What I would not pitch or build

AI judgment of presentation quality (juries have seen a hundred of these and your instinct about reliability is right), slide editing or an all-in-one suite, polls and quizzes, LMS integrations in year one, hardware.

## Application narrative skeleton

1. **Problem.** Every in-room presentation still runs on lost clickers, borrowed laptops and a laser nobody can see; audiences cannot follow; presenters have no record of what they said.
2. **Solution.** The phone you already own becomes the remote, the pointer, the spotlight, the notes and the recorder, on every slide tool and every computer, with nothing to install when needed.
3. **USP.** The always-connects architecture (five paths, nothing to install), the Logitech Spotlight experience without the €130 device, and a privacy-clean design (no accounts, on-device speech).
4. **Market.** Hardware clicker sales as the beachhead proxy (about 30,000 units a month from the top ten Amazon US listings alone), the audience-interaction market as the expansion (Mentimeter and Slido at tens of millions of euros a year), education as the first channel (more oral presentations because of AI-written homework).
5. **Validation.** 46,000 installs and 2.6% paid conversion of the 2021 prototype with no marketing; the current proofs of concept on six platforms; launch-phase numbers; letters of intent.
6. **Business model.** Free with credit; Pro one-time; Pro+ subscription for transcription and captions; room and event licenses for institutions and organizers.
7. **Competition.** PPTControl (needs its app, 3.1 stars on Android), free first-party remotes (Keynote only, Slides by casting), hardware (no effects under €100), dead first-party apps (demand proven, nobody serving it).
8. **Team.** You, with the two gaps named (marketing, sales) and the plan for them (co-founder search through the university's matching, a working student in year two).
9. **Milestones.** The table above, aims column.
10. **After the stipend.** Revenue-funded by default; GründungsBONUS Berlin or a small angel round only if room mode and audience features show pull.

## Follow-up funding after the stipend (low confidence on details; check each)

GründungsBONUS Berlin (a grant for young innovative Berlin companies, up to around €50,000), IBB's Pro FIT for development-heavy projects, Berlin's accelerator programmes, and angels only if institutional pull exists. None needs a decision now; naming one in the application shows you have thought past month 12.

## What I am not sure about

- Room mode pricing and sales cycle. I have no data; the pilots will tell you.
- Whether universities will install a solo founder's software on lecture PCs. The privacy-clean design and Microsoft-signed, notarized packages help; a professor's backing helps more.
- Audience features. The market is crowded and the cost is high; it may be right to leave them as "where this goes" rather than build them in year one.
- The aim numbers are meant to be ambitious. If you want a safer application, halve the aims and keep the expectations.
